## 3. How often accounts post (the scheduling and cost model)

Posts per day over the last year (or over what was read for capped accounts), for the 257 accounts with posts:

| p10 | p25 | median | p75 | p90 | mean | max |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.06 | 0.21 | **0.61** | 1.8 | 4.3 | **3.2** | 132 |

- **164 of 257 accounts post less than once a day**; 25 post 5 or more times a day; 8 post 24 or more times a day (the news channels).
- So most new-post checks find nothing. A check interval set by each account's posting rate keeps the 99 %-within-3-hours target (D2 counts posts, not accounts) at a fraction of the calls of checking everyone every 2 h 45 min.
- The one-year backfill of this list took **7,414 list pages for 104,045 posts** (14 a page); 28 busy accounts would need more than 100 pages, and their pages can be read as time slices in parallel (the cursor is a time).

## 4. What went wrong, and the fixes (M1j)

- **TikTok errors: 11 of about 9,000 list calls (0.12 %)** answered `statusCode` 10102 (9) or 10101 (2, with HTTP 500). The same identity's next calls succeeded every time, so these are passing server errors, not blocks. Eight of them stopped a walk. **Fix:** the walk asks a failed page again once after a pause; the crawler will retry with backoff.
- **"Stuck": 4 walks** stopped on a page that held only the post at the cursor (the oldest post of the page before). TikTok answers only a window of time before the cursor (yt-dlp reports about a week), so after a gap in an account's posting the page holds just that post. **Fix:** the walk moves the cursor past it and goes on; only 3 such pages in a row stop it. The fake TikTok now has the window and the errors, and a test walks through both.
- **An empty first page** (one account quiet for longer than the window) skipped the walk. **Fix:** the walk starts whenever the first page says older posts remain.

## 6. What it means

- **The plain-HTTP part of the method holds at the scale of the real list**: 273 accounts, a year back, 10 IPs, an hour, no captcha. Accounts, posts, metrics, pinned flags and photo posts all come from the `creator` list.
- **T20, first look:** about 1,100 requests per IP per hour for an hour drew no captcha or block. Phase 0 (C4) measures how long that lasts and where it breaks, by raising the rate in steps.
- **The 300,000-account arithmetic gets cheaper.** With this list's posting rates (mean 3.2 posts a day, median 0.6), checks timed by posting rate need far fewer calls than one per account every 2 h 45 min. The backfill of a new account is a few to a hundred pages.
- **Crawler rules from this pass:**
  - retry 10101/10102 with backoff;
  - move past a page holding only the cursor's post;
  - step back on an empty page while older posts remain;
  - date posts by `createTime` and find new ones by id;
  - treat 10221 and 209002 shells as unavailable accounts.
