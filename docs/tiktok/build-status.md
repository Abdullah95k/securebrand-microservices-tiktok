From test pass run 3 and the full pass (2026-11-01; [run 3](c2-findings-2026-10-31.md#10-run-3-2026-11-01), [full pass](c3-findings-2026-11-01.md)):

  - all 273 accounts, 104,045 posts with exact counts;
  - 11,509 requests in an hour through 10 IPs (about 1,100 per IP-hour) with no captcha or challenge;
  - accounts read to their first post gave a median 97.8 % of the videos their profile counts.
- **Comments and replies come through the signer.** 277 comments and 28 replies were read from the page, page after page. A signed URL works by plain HTTP from a fresh identity, and still works 14–84 s later. A changed cursor breaks it, so each request needs its own signature.

- **Most accounts post rarely:** median 0.6 posts a day, mean 3.2; 164 of 257 post less than daily, 8 post 24 or more times a day. Checks timed by posting rate cut the calls a lot.
- **TikTok answers a window before the cursor, and errs now and then (0.12 %).** The walk moves past pages that hold only the cursor's post, and retries the errors.

From the first probe test pass (C2 run 1, 2026-10-31, US ISP tier; details in [`c2-findings-2026-10-31.md`](c2-findings-2026-10-31.md)):
- **Plain HTTP works for pages and the post list.** 5 of 5 profile pages loaded through the ISP tier with no captcha or challenge, 4 with the whole account. The `creator` list answered **unsigned, without cookies, device id or `msToken`**, 15 complete posts a page (~40 KB). Its cursor is a time that works from any identity.
- **Comments need TikTok's own script.** Unsigned, they were refused. In run 1, signed by the page with Chrome's automation flag on, still refused. **In run 2, with the flag off, answered (4/4), and the signed URL also worked by plain HTTP from another identity on the same IP.** The app's own list call stays refused from the page with tks's URLs, but the `creator` list carries everything, including `isPinnedItem`.
- **Run 2 read the lists properly:** 3,116 unique posts; one account to its first post (567 of 580 videos); pages answered at 30–730 days back for every account; at most 15 posts a page.
- **Scheduled posts:** on two news channels most posts went public 1.5–2.7 h after their id was made, so new posts are found by id set and dated by `createTime`.
- **Video files answered 403**; images download freely. A browser profile page costs ~6.7 MB with media blocked.
- From a US exit TikTok serves its US cluster (`region: US`, `clusterRegion: TTP`).

**Speed at production scale** (the owner's question, 2026-11-01). The probe is slow on purpose: one request every ~3 s, one account at a time, ~70 diagnostic requests per account. Production makes **one `creator` call per account per check**: its items carry the posts with metrics and the account (nickname, bio, avatar, verified, private) with its counts (`authorStatsV2`), so the same call refreshes the account; only the bio link needs the page (weekly). The calls run in parallel over many identities. For 300,000 accounts: about 9 checks a day, ≈ 3.2 M calls a day (~37 a second), ~120 identities busy at once at a person's pace. Speed is therefore not the limit. **The open number is calls per IP per day (T20)**, which sets the IP count: ~1,600 IPs at 2,000 a day, ~640 at 5,000, ~160 at 20,000. Further levers: the one-year backfill read as time slices in parallel (the cursor is a time and portable), check intervals by posting rate (the 99 % target counts posts), `count=5` for routine checks (~17 KB instead of ~40 KB), browser pages kept open and used only for comments. The full pass (10 at a time) gives a first look at T20; Phase 0 measures it with a rate ladder.

From the owner's recordings (2026-10-31; details in [`har-findings-2026-10-31.md`](har-findings-2026-10-31.md)):
- **A logged-out browser in Iraq got everything the objective asks for**: the account (with the bio link, exact counts in `statsV2`, account creation and last rename times), the post list page by page with counts, pinned and ad flags and photo posts, **top-level comments and replies**.
- **Every call was signed by TikTok's script** (`X-Gnarly`, `X-Dynosaur`; `X-Bogus` was the constant `1`), and the list call was the app's own `/api/post/item_list/`. Whether a crawler gets the same unsigned is now the main question (T4), for the probe.
- **Comments come in TikTok's ranking order** (T13): refreshes re-read to the cap. One post read to the end had 96 % of its count in top-level comments plus replies.
- **Post ids carry their time** (T8), so backfills stop at 365 days from ids.
- **One busy account posts about every 15 minutes**: backfills and comments of such accounts dominate the cost.
- **Comment authors carry `predicted_age_group`**: tks never stores it (ADR 0007 §6).
- The export stripped cookies; post pages were not recorded. Both are for the probe.

From the kickoff research (public sources only; details and citations in [`methods.md`](methods.md)):
- **No official TikTok API fits a commercial monitor of third-party accounts.** The Research API is for non-profit academic researchers in the US, EEA, UK and Switzerland, forbids commercial use, allows 1,000 requests a day, and lags up to 48 h for new videos and 10 days for counts. The Display and Business APIs cover only accounts that authorise the app. The Commercial Content API covers ads in Europe only.
- **Third-party providers charging per call** are cheap at small scale (about $0.01 to $0.03 per account per day for this workload) but reach about $70k to $135k a month at 300,000 accounts; per-result pricing (Apify, Bright Data) costs 10 to 30 times more for this re-polling workload. Using one is the owner's decision (D4).
- **The public evidence favours the simple method for the post list:** yt-dlp and gallery-dl read whole post lists through an unsigned call (`/api/creator/item_list/`) with a random device id and no browser, as of August 2026. The app's own list call needs `X-Gnarly`, and the account call may need a signature (T4).
- **Comments are the biggest risk:** public reports conflict on whether TikTok serves them to logged-out visitors at all (T12). The recordings (R2) and the probe's test pass settle it before the crawler is built.
- **TikTok blocks more than igs's target does:** TLS and header fingerprints, a region-dependent JavaScript challenge since late 2025, IP reputation, captchas, burnt identities and device ids (T5, T6, T28, T29). Empty 200 answers are the usual failure, so tks counts every empty answer as a failure.
- **igs's scraping layer fits TikTok with three seams** (GET and a decoder, registry variants, a persistent browser page); its probe, fake site and isolation guards carry over (`reuse-plan.md`).
