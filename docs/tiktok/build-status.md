## Findings

From M5c (2026-11-01, no TikTok request):
- **A check's page and list reads arrive together, so the writer applies every account record in order.** Keeping only the newest per account in a batch lost the page's exact counts and bio link to the list author read seconds later; the end-to-end test caught it.
- **Metrics rows are written only on change**: a quiet check that re-reads every post on its pages wrote no `post_metrics` row, and a check after three posts' counts changed wrote three (plus one for a new post). Storage for metrics grows with change, not with checks.
- **`fakett`'s list author now carries the bio** (`signature`) and the private flag, as the recordings show TikTok's does.

From M5b (2026-11-01, no TikTok request):
- **A signature costs browser time, not a request.** The signer page builds the signed URL with TikTok's own script and never sends it; the worker that asked sends it by plain HTTP. Each comment page (20 comments) and each reply page (3 replies) is one signature, so replies cost the most per item: a thread is read only when its reply count grew, and a comment whose one reply TikTok quotes inline needs no thread read.
- **Comments are re-read whole when a post's count goes up** (TikTok's ranking order, T13). The cap per post (`tt.comments.max_per_post`, 1000 now; after Phase 0) is therefore the main cost lever for busy posts.
- **Comments need `tt.signer.mode: browser`**: with `none` TikTok refuses every comment call, and the tasks end in the dead letter queue (the posts are then read whole next time). Whether a page on one account signs well for every account (T32) is for Phase 0 to measure.

From M5a (2026-11-01, no TikTok request):
- **A list check costs one call per 15 posts and refreshes them all**: every post on a page read is published with its counts, also those past the watermark on the last page. An account that posted nothing since the last check costs one call.
- **The daily account refresh reads the profile page** (one page per account per day, ~92 KB on the wire in the recordings), because only the page has exact counts (T31) and the bio link; the list's author covers renames, bio and avatar changes on every check at no cost.
- **Pinned posts come at their own date on the `creator` list**, so the watermark needs no exemption for them; a post pinned long after it was posted gets its flag from the next metrics walk that reaches its date.

From M4 (2026-11-01, no TikTok request; from the HARs and run 3's raw answers):
- **Large counts are rounded, even in `statsV2`** (new T31). A post's counts of 10,000 and more come rounded to 3 or 4 significant digits, on the list and the post page alike; a list item's `authorStatsV2` is rounded too. Only the profile page's account counts are exact. So the creator list refreshes the account's name, bio, avatar and flags on every check, but its **exact counts come from the daily page read**, and a large post's metrics change only at the rounding step. The parsers mark list-sourced account counts as not exact; ADR 0007 §8 and the `PostCounts` comment are corrected.
- **The parsers read every fixture without drift:** no required field missing in 18 answers of 7 kinds from two countries' services (Iraq's recordings, the probe's US exit).
- **Video links on lists can be `www.tiktok.com/aweme/v1/play/` redirects** carrying `signature=` and `tk=tt_chain_token`, and some commenters' avatars are on Facebook's CDN. The scrubber treats both as signed or foreign links.

**Speed at production scale** (the owner's question, 2026-11-01). The probe is slow on purpose: one request every ~3 s, one account at a time, ~70 diagnostic requests per account. Production makes **one `creator` call per account per check**: its items carry the posts with metrics and the account (nickname, bio, avatar, verified, private) with its counts (`authorStatsV2`), so the same call refreshes the account; only the bio link needs the page (weekly). The calls run in parallel over many identities. For 300,000 accounts: about 9 checks a day, ≈ 3.2 M calls a day (~37 a second), ~120 identities busy at once at a person's pace. Speed is therefore not the limit. **The open number is calls per IP per day (T20)**, which sets the IP count: ~1,600 IPs at 2,000 a day, ~640 at 5,000, ~160 at 20,000. Further levers: the one-year backfill read as time slices in parallel (the cursor is a time and portable), check intervals by posting rate (the 99 % target counts posts), `count=5` for routine checks (~17 KB instead of ~40 KB), browser pages kept open and used only for comments. The full pass (10 at a time) gives a first look at T20; Phase 0 measures it with a rate ladder.

From the kickoff research (public sources only; details and citations in [`methods.md`](methods.md)):
- **No official TikTok API fits a commercial monitor of third-party accounts.** The Research API is for non-profit academic researchers in the US, EEA, UK and Switzerland, forbids commercial use, allows 1,000 requests a day, and lags up to 48 h for new videos and 10 days for counts. The Display and Business APIs cover only accounts that authorise the app. The Commercial Content API covers ads in Europe only.
- **Third-party providers charging per call** are cheap at small scale (about $0.01 to $0.03 per account per day for this workload) but reach about $70k to $135k a month at 300,000 accounts; per-result pricing (Apify, Bright Data) costs 10 to 30 times more for this re-polling workload. Using one is the owner's decision (D4).
- **The public evidence favours the simple method for the post list:** yt-dlp and gallery-dl read whole post lists through an unsigned call (`/api/creator/item_list/`) with a random device id and no browser, as of August 2026. The app's own list call needs `X-Gnarly`, and the account call may need a signature (T4).
- **Comments are the biggest risk:** public reports conflict on whether TikTok serves them to logged-out visitors at all (T12). The recordings (R2) and the probe's test pass settle it before the crawler is built.
- **TikTok blocks more than igs's target does:** TLS and header fingerprints, a region-dependent JavaScript challenge since late 2025, IP reputation, captchas, burnt identities and device ids (T5, T6, T28, T29). Empty 200 answers are the usual failure, so tks counts every empty answer as a failure.
- **igs's scraping layer fits TikTok with three seams** (GET and a decoder, registry variants, a persistent browser page); its probe, fake site and isolation guards carry over (`reuse-plan.md`).
